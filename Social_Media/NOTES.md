## Note 1: Unit of Action - Clarification

**Date:** October 28, 2026

### The Question

What counts as a single "action" subject to TML's No Log = No Action precondition?

The report uses "10-100 billion ranking decisions per day" and "approximately 115,000 decisions per second at peak" as the operational scale figures for Meta. These figures are accurate as measures of impression volume - the number of times content is served to individual users. However, they conflate two distinct concepts: the amplification decision and its downstream consequences.

### The Correct Definition

The unit of action under TML governance is the **amplification decision** - the moment a platform chooses to push content beyond its organic reach to a defined audience category.

This is a singular, traceable act. It is the moral act TML is designed to govern.

Individual impressions served to users are consequences of that amplification decision, not separate moral acts. When a platform decides to amplify a piece of content to 10 million users, it has made one governance decision - not 10 million.

An analogy: a novel is one creative act. The number of readers who respond to Chapter 3 differently than Chapter 17 does not multiply the author's original act of writing into thousands of separate acts. The readers experience consequences; the author made one decision.

### What This Changes

**Architecture:** Nothing. Sacred Zero fires when moral ambiguity is detected at the amplification decision point. Whether the audience is 10 users or 10 million, the trigger logic is identical. All eight pillars remain unchanged.

**Scale estimates:** The impression-level figures (10-100 billion/day, 115,000/second) overstate the governance load. The correct volume is the number of distinct amplification decisions made per day - a figure that is large but materially smaller than impression count, and one that platforms have not publicly disclosed in disaggregated form.

**Sacred Pause economics:** The 500:1 attack-to-defense cost ratio identified in Section C assumed impression-level Sacred Pause triggers. At amplification-decision level, the DoS attack surface is meaningfully reduced. The 500:1 ratio remains a valid concern but the absolute numbers require recalibration against actual amplification decision volume rather than impression volume.

**Human reviewer workforce:** The 100,000 reviewer estimate derived from a 0.1% Sacred Zero trigger rate applied to impression volume. Applied to amplification-decision volume, the workforce requirement is lower. The structural argument - that the workforce does not currently exist and must be built - remains valid regardless of the exact figure.

### What This Does Not Change

The core finding stands: platforms are structurally unwilling rather than unable. The EU DSA mandate pathway exists. TML's architecture is technically achievable. The conditional verdict is unchanged.

### The One-Line Answer to the Obvious Critic

"The unit is the amplification decision. Everything downstream of that decision is its consequence, not a separate moral act."

**Date:** October 2026

No revenue loss projection appears in this report. Any citation of a "$13 billion" figure in reference to this work is not sourced from this research.
