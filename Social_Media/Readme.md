# Applied Research: Social Media

**Can TML Reform Social Media?**
*Can Algorithmic Governance Be Constitutionalized?*

## About This Report

This report stress-tests the Ternary Moral Logic (TML) framework against one of the hardest available cases: the feed ranking and amplification architecture of Very Large Online Platforms (VLOPs).

It was written by the framework's creator - not as a validation exercise, but as a deliberate adversarial audit. The opening question frames the entire analysis:

> *If not TML, then what?*

Every platform audit, every parliamentary inquiry, every wrongful death lawsuit eventually arrives at the same silence: no one can name what a governed feed ranking system actually looks like at scale. This report is an attempt to fill that silence.

See `NOTES.md` for clarifications and corrections added after initial submission.

## Key Findings

**Technical achievability:** TML's Dual-Lane Latency architecture - Inference Lane under 2ms, Governance Lane under 50ms - is consistent with existing infrastructure at Polygon (5,000+ TPS) and analogous to Visa's real-time fraud detection at 65,000 TPS. The engineering is not the barrier.

**Economic realism:** The Governance Lane costs approximately $50-100 million per year at Meta scale. Measured against the $52 million PTSD settlement covering just 11,250 content moderators, or the November 2025 Common Sense Media finding of "Unacceptable Risk" across four of eight child safety principles for AI systems on the same engagement-first architecture - the cost of TML is a measurement. The cost of the status quo is a burial.

**Platform willingness:** Platforms are structurally unwilling, not structurally unable. The mandate paradox - every major framework stops short of requiring structural change - reflects the precise point at which governance would constrain the business model.

**Regulatory pathway:** The EU DSA pathway exists. Under Articles 34 and 51, the European Commission has authority to mandate Governance Lane-equivalent architecture from VLOPs. This is the highest-feasibility jurisdiction and the narrowest viable window.

**Conditional verdict:** TML can reform social media feed ranking - under mandate, within the EU jurisdiction, with Tri-Cameral governance intact, and with human review preserved at scale.

## Most Serious Vulnerabilities Identified

**1. Sacred Pause attack surface (500:1 economics)**
A coordinated campaign generating borderline content at ~$1,000/day can impose ~$500,000/day in human review costs. TML's current specification does not include tiered review or confidence-weighted escalation mitigations. This is the most operationally serious vulnerability. See `NOTES.md` for a scale recalibration following clarification of the unit of action.

**2. Governance capture without constitutional specification**
The Tri-Cameral Governance structure (Technical Council, Ethics Chamber, Stewardship Custodians) requires explicit constitutional specification of independence, appointment, and removal mechanisms. Every historical governance body without these specifications has been captured by the interests it was meant to oversee.

**3. Human moderator workforce does not exist**
At the Sacred Zero trigger rate applied to amplification-decision volume, TML requires a trained ethical reviewer workforce that does not currently exist. Building it is a precondition for TML's full implementation, not a downstream consequence of it. See `NOTES.md` for revised framing of the scale estimate.

## Unit of Action - Key Definition

The unit of action under TML governance is the **amplification decision** - the moment a platform chooses to push content beyond its organic reach to a defined audience. Individual impressions served are consequences of that decision, not separate moral acts.

This definition is foundational to all scale, cost, and workforce estimates in the report. See `NOTES.md` (Note 1) for the full clarification and its implications.
